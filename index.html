The question

Two questions about your own community are surprisingly hard to answer.

When one of your members needed something — did this room deliver?

And: what is already possible between the people here that nobody has put together yet?

You can tell a good story about both. Neither one can be shown.

The gap

Your community isn't disengaged. It's quiet.

Wayne Baker at Michigan Ross puts a number on it: 70–90% of the help given in a workplace comes only in response to a direct request. People are willing. They just don't know who needs what.

And members don't ask. Across studies in which researchers made requests of more than 14,000 people, we underestimate how likely others are to say yes — by 48% on average — so we decide in advance that it isn't worth asking, and stay silent.

Most of a community's value sits in that gap. Not a shortage of goodwill. A shortage of asking.

The mechanism

Asking becomes normal — and the ask reaches the right hands.

A member asks for something specific: someone who has done X, advice on a stuck decision, a recommendation, an introduction. One line, thirty seconds.

A chat shouts at everyone. The graph knows who.

The ask doesn't drop into a feed of two hundred people and sink in twenty minutes — it reaches the three people whose experience is actually relevant and who are close enough to answer. That is what the map underneath is for.

The map underneath — who is close, who has drifted

Answering is deliberately cheap: I can help · I know someone · not me — try here. Because silence is worse than a no.

And the first move isn't a request. A member can start by offering something small — five minutes, an introduction, a look at someone's deck. They enter as someone who has already given.

The record

Every thank-you is a record.

"Thank you, closed" isn't politeness. It's a record: this person helped, and the person who received the help confirmed it. One tap.

Enough records, and the community stops being a feeling and becomes an account. Who actually gave what. Which introductions landed. Where help moves freely, and where it gets stuck.

A contact card — trust, balance, and what actually happened

Which finally produces the two numbers every community sells and none can measure:

→What share of member Asks got an answer.
→How long the first answer took.

That's the honest version of the promise you make to everyone considering joining. It's what you show instead of a deck. And it warns you a month early when something has gone wrong.

What we're not

We're not a club.

A club gives people a circle, selection, gatherings and shared experience. No tool replaces that, and we don't compete for it.

What a club can't do is show what is actually happening inside. Everyone has a sense of access. Nobody has measurable mutual help.

We're the part that's missing.

What you get

This picture is yours — and here is what we won't do.

Members see no ranking, no points, no leaderboard. There won't be one.

You get something else: an operator's view of your own room, so you can steward it deliberately instead of guessing. Who to thank. Who to pull closer. Who is drifting and could be brought back this month rather than at renewal. Which introduction is worth making this week.

Signals — the operator's view of your room

The boundaries we hold, because your members trust you when they walk in:

We don't sell information about people. Not a policy that could change — the thing the company is built on.

Nobody messages on anyone's behalf. AI can draft. A person always sends.

No feed, no likes, no public score.

A member's network belongs to them. They leave, it leaves with them. It was never ours.

Contribution is recorded, never claimed. What's visible is what happened, confirmed by the person who received the help.

How we start

How the first cohorts start.

A small number of partner communities, built together with their leaders rather than handed a login. We start with you, and your community enters at once — so members arrive already connected to people they know, and the map has something to show on the first screen instead of an empty product.

What we're asking for

Thirty minutes about how help actually moves in your community today. Who gets asked. Who never does. What happens when nobody answers.

If it fits, we build the first cohort together.
