The category

Every community tool answers one question: who is here?

Directories store profiles, feeds distribute information, CRMs track leads. None of them work with the layer underneath — who knows whom, who can help whom, what has actually happened between them. That layer is the community's social capital, and today it is invisible, unmanaged and unmeasured.

The insight

Wayne Baker at Michigan Ross puts a number on the gap: 70–90% of the help given in a workplace comes only in response to a direct request.1

And across studies in which researchers made requests of more than 14,000 people, we underestimate how likely others are to say yes by 48% on average2 — so we don't ask at all.

A community can hold enormous goodwill and expertise while almost none of it moves.

That isn't a motivation problem. It's a routing problem.

The mechanism

A member posts an Ask — one line, thirty seconds.

A chat shouts at everyone; the graph knows who.

Selfspace uses the community's relationship structure to route the Ask to the few people whose experience and proximity make them likely to answer, and makes answering cheap: I can help · I know someone · not me, try here. When help lands, the person who received it confirms it in one tap. That confirmation is the unit the whole system is built on: contribution recorded, never claimed.

The relationship graph — trust and gravity behind every ask
What it produces

Enough confirmed traces and the community becomes structure you can read — clusters, bridges, the connectors help actually travels through, the gaps, and the pairs who could build something neither would have found alone.

Structure you can read — clusters, bridges, and gaps

Plus the two numbers every community sells and none can measure:

→What share of member Asks got an answer.
→How long the first answer took.

Community health measured as whether value moves between people — not attendance, likes or engagement scores.

Why it holds

The data isn't scraped or self-reported. It's generated by the community's own use: confirmed help events on top of a relationship graph.

It compounds, and it doesn't transfer.

And we're not competing with clubs. A club gives circle, selection and shared experience, and no tool replaces that. What a club can't do is show what's happening inside — everyone has a sense of access, nobody has measurable mutual help. We sit underneath whatever the community already uses for chat, content and events.

A confirmed record — trust and balance generated by actual use
What we don't do

The lines we don't cross — because this kind of data is only lent once.

We don't sell information about people.

AI can draft, a person always sends.

No feed, no likes, no public ranking of human beings.

A member's network belongs to them and leaves with them.

Where we are
Closed beta

Working with a small number of partner communities.

We enter alongside the community leader and the community enters at once — cohorts rather than isolated sign-ups — so members arrive already connected and the map has something to show on day one. That entry sequence is specified, down to the data model; what's left is running it with a live room.
